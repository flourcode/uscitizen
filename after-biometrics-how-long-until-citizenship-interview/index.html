Biometrics done. How long until the interview?

Nothing visible happens for a while after biometrics, and that silence makes people nervous. Here is what is actually going on and how long it usually takes.

What biometrics was for

Your fingerprints and photo go to the FBI for a background check, and your name is checked against other government databases. This is routine and takes days to a few weeks. If you got a notice saying USCIS would reuse fingerprints from an earlier application, the same check happens without the appointment.

How long the wait is

The interview is scheduled by your field office in the order applications were filed, so the wait depends almost entirely on which office has your case. In recent years most applicants have been interviewed several months after biometrics, with a range from a few weeks to well over a year at the slowest offices. The only reliable number is the one for your office at egov.uscis.gov/processing-times; enter N-400 and your field office.

What the status messages mean

  • Case was received / Fingerprint fee was received: normal, nothing to do.
  • Biometrics appointment was scheduled / Fingerprints were taken: you are here.
  • Case is being actively reviewed: an officer has looked at the file. Often appears weeks before an interview is scheduled, but not always.
  • Interview was scheduled: the notice is in the mail. Check your online account; the date shows there first.
  • Request for evidence: USCIS needs a document. Respond by the deadline in the letter.

When to ask what's happening

Not before the posted processing time has passed; inquiries before that are answered with a form letter. After it has passed, submit a case inquiry online, and if that goes nowhere, contact your U.S. representative's office, which has a direct line to USCIS and helps constituents with stuck cases as a matter of routine.

Use the wait

This is the best study time you will have, with no pressure. One round a day in the trainer for two weeks covers all 128 questions; after that, a review round a few times a week keeps them fresh for months. Read the N-400 questions once so you know what the officer will ask, and put the document list somewhere you'll find it.

Things that can slow it down

  • Moving to a different field office's area (file AR-11, then expect a transfer delay).
  • A name check that takes longer than usual. Nothing to do but wait.
  • Travel abroad that USCIS wants to ask about. Keep your trips short and documented.

Last reviewed September 5, 2026. Procedures vary by field office and change over time; confirm anything that matters for your case at uscis.gov.

Ready when you are.

Use the wait. Ten minutes a day is enough.
