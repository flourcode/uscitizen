The questions people miss

Most of the 128 are easy. These fifteen are where interviews go wrong, usually because the question has a long list of answers or a number with no story. Each one comes with a way to remember it.

#81 · Colonial period and independence
There were 13 original states. Name five.

Thirteen states, and you need five. Most people freeze after New York and Massachusetts.

Remember it: Picture the East Coast from top to bottom: New Hampshire, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, Connecticut, New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Delaware, Maryland, Virginia, North Carolina, South Carolina, Georgia. Practice saying the same five every time: New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Virginia, Georgia.

#83 · Colonial period and independence
The Federalist Papers supported the passage of the U.S. Constitution. Name one of the writers.

Nobody outside a history class knows who wrote the Federalist Papers.

Remember it: Three names and a pen name: Madison, Hamilton, Jay, and Publius. Say "Hamilton" and stop; one is all the question asks for.

#48 · System of government
What are two Cabinet-level positions?

Twenty-two cabinet positions, and the question wants two.

Remember it: Don't learn the list. Learn two: Secretary of State and Secretary of Defense. Give those two, every time.

#7 · Principles of government
How many amendments does the U.S. Constitution have?

A bare number with no story attached.

Remember it: Twenty-seven. The Bill of Rights is the first ten; seventeen more came later.

#97 · The 1800s
What amendment says all persons born or naturalized in the United States, and subject to the jurisdiction thereof, are U.S. citizens?

Three amendments from the Civil War era, one question.

Remember it: 13 ended slavery, 14 made citizens, 15 gave the vote. This question is the middle one: the 14th.

#127 · National holidays
What is Memorial Day?

Memorial Day and Veterans Day sound the same.

Remember it: Memorial Day honors those who died in service (May). Veterans Day honors everyone who served (November).

#22 · System of government
How long is a term for a U.S. senator?

Three different term lengths in three different questions.

Remember it: Senators 6, House 2, President 4. Say it as a chant until it's automatic.

#53 · System of government
How many seats are on the Supreme Court?

Two numbers for the Supreme Court and they're easy to swap.

Remember it: Nine seats, five to decide. Nine on the bench, five for a majority.

#28 · System of government
Why does each state have two senators?

A history-class answer to a question that sounds simple.

Remember it: Each state has two senators for equal representation for small states; that deal was the Great Compromise. Either phrase works.

#110 · Recent history
Why did the United States enter the Korean War?

Two wars, and the answer is the same for both.

Remember it: Korea and Vietnam: "to stop the spread of communism." One answer, two questions.

#58 · System of government
Name one power that is only for the federal government.

Federal versus state powers get mixed up under pressure.

Remember it: Money, war, and other countries are federal (print money, declare war, create an army, make treaties). Schools, police, fire, driver's licenses are state.

#14 · Principles of government
Many documents influenced the U.S. Constitution. Name one.

Eight possible answers, most of them unfamiliar.

Remember it: Pick the one you already know: the Declaration of Independence. It counts.

#124 · American symbols
The Nation’s first motto was “E Pluribus Unum.” What does that mean?

Latin.

Remember it: E Pluribus Unum: "out of many, one." It's on every coin in your pocket.

#98 · The 1800s
When did all men get the right to vote?

A date question where the date isn't the best answer.

Remember it: When did all men get the vote? Say "with the 15th Amendment" or "after the Civil War." You don't need the year (1870).

#30 · System of government
What is the name of the Speaker of the House of Representatives now?

Not hard to learn, easy to have wrong.

Remember it: The Speaker of the House changes more often than any other name on the test. Check it the week of your interview.

The pattern

Almost every hard question is hard for one of two reasons: the answer is a bare number, or the question has a long list of acceptable answers and you try to learn the list. The fix is the same both times. Attach a story to the number, and pick one answer from the list and never think about the others again.

Drill just these: in the trainer, "Review what you've missed" will naturally fill with them after your first pass through the deck.
