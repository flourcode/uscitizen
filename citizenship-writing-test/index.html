The citizenship writing test

The officer says a sentence, you write it. Every sentence is built from this official vocabulary list.

How it works. The officer reads a sentence aloud, usually the answer to the sentence you just read. You write it on a tablet or paper. You get up to three tries and need one correct. Spelling and capitalization need to be close enough that the officer can read the same meaning; a missed comma will not fail you.

Typical sentences. "Washington was the first President." "Citizens can vote." "The White House is in Washington, D.C." "Independence Day is in July."
