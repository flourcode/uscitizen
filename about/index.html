About CitizenshipPal

A free study site for the U.S. citizenship test, built by one person who thinks the interview should be the least scary part of becoming an American.

Mark FlournoyBuilder of CitizenshipPal. Retired sales leader, former U.S. Marine. LinkedIn

Hello

I'm Mark. I'm 58, I spent the last stretch of my career leading sales teams at Amazon, and before any of that I was a United States Marine. I retired, took a breath, and started thinking about what I'd actually enjoy doing next. This site is the answer.

The people I worked with over those years came from everywhere: India, Mexico, Nigeria, Vietnam, Ukraine, the Philippines, a dozen more. Some of the best thinking I ever saw came from colleagues who had learned to solve problems in one country and then learned to solve them again, differently, in this one. Several of them went through the citizenship process while we worked together. I watched smart, capable people lose sleep over a twenty-minute conversation and a list of questions any of us could learn in a couple of weeks. That stuck with me.

Why the interview is worth getting right

Here's the thing nobody tells you: the civics test is fair. The questions are published. The officer wants you to pass. What trips people up is not the material, it's the format: a stranger asking you questions out loud, in English, while you're nervous. So the trainer on this site makes you practice the way the test is given. You hear the question. You say the answer. Then you check. Do that for ten minutes a day for a few weeks and the interview becomes a formality, which is exactly what it should be.

I took an oath myself at eighteen, in a room full of people I'd never met, and I still remember the words. When you say yours, I'd like you to be thinking about what they mean and not about whether you remembered how many amendments there are. That's the whole idea.

What's here

  • All 128 questions and accepted answers from the 2025 USCIS civics test, in official order, each with its own page, a memory hook, and a button that reads it aloud.
  • The Citizenship Interview Trainer: short rounds, questions spoken aloud, and a practice test scored the way the officer scores it.
  • Practice for the rest of the interview: the N-400 questions asked out loud, the English reading and writing tests, and plain-English guides to what happens on the day.
  • Instructions in Spanish, Chinese, Vietnamese, and Arabic, the Spanish text of every question, and a classroom mode for teachers.

Where the content comes from, and how it's kept right

The questions, the accepted answers, and the reading and writing vocabulary come from documents published by U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services, which are public domain. I check them against the current USCIS publication whenever it changes, and the names of current officials are updated after every election or appointment, with the date shown on each page. The explanations, the memory hooks, the guides, and the trainer are my own work. Native speakers review the language pages. If you find an error, tell me; corrections are the most useful mail I get.

What I am not

I'm not a lawyer, and this site is not legal advice. I'm not affiliated with USCIS, the Department of Homeland Security, or any government agency. I can help you learn the material and know what to expect; I can't tell you whether to file, how to answer a hard question on your N-400, or what a "yes" on one of the "have you ever" questions means for your case. For those, talk to an immigration attorney or an accredited representative. Free and low-cost help is listed at uscis.gov.

How the site is paid for

The study pages carry a small amount of advertising. The trainer has none and never will; nobody should be interrupted mid-question. There are no accounts, no email lists, and nothing to buy. If it helps you or someone you love, the best thing you can do is pass, and then tell the next person.
